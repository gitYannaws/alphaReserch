## Key question 1: What exactly does Sales Safari tell you to record?

### Takeaway
The canonical note structure has four categories, which Alex Hillman calls the "core quadrants": pain, jargon, worldview, and recommendations, collected in separate passes over the same material (pain first). The 30x500 lesson template adds URL and date per session, and the authors also say to capture what the audience buys, the tools they use, and where they gather ("watering holes"), but those are taught as audience-selection and source-finding inputs rather than as separate note columns.

### Cited Findings

**The four note categories (the method's own words)**
- Alex Hillman on the origin and categories: "So I want to start by making sure that Amy has credit for inventing Sales Safari." and the note structure was built when early students reported finding nothing in forums; Hillman found "many didn't take notes," so the team "created a note-taking structure and taught it as a skill" *(summary of transcript)*; the four categories are "pain, jargon, worldview, and recommendations." — [Stacking the Bricks podcast EP42, "What is Sales Safari?" with Étienne Garbugli, 5 Nov 2020](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Hillman, same episode: the method is "100% her brain baby" (Amy Hoy's). — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Passes are sequential by category: "Write down all the pain you see." then "Then do it again and look for jargon." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Hillman in the Lean B2B interview: the core categories are "pain, jargon, worldview, and recommendations" plus "a few others that show up from time to time" (he does not name the others). — [Lean B2B, "Alex Hillman on How a Sales Safari Can Help You Find a Business Opportunity in B2B", undated, c. 2020](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Stacking the Bricks' own summary of the note sheet: "The note categories are pain, jargon, worldview, and recommendations. You make one pass per category, starting with looking for pain." *(search snippet only; the search engine attributed it to stackingthebricks.com without a page URL)* — [search result set for "stackingthebricks Sales Safari how to"](https://stackingthebricks.com/articles/)

**The 30x500 lesson's session template (the method's own words)**
- Definition: "Sales Safari is the process of learning what naturally attracts and motivates people 'out in the wild.'" — [30x500 lesson "Sales Safari in a Nutshell" hosted on egghead.io, undated](https://egghead.io/learn/30x500/sales-safari)
- The "Session Note Template" has five fields: URL, date, pain, jargon, recommendations. Field definitions: Pain: "The posters' pains (current, past, implied)"; Jargon: "Terms of art, technical lingo, community phrases"; Recommendations: "Advice what to buy, read, share, try... or NOT to". — [egghead.io 30x500 lesson](https://egghead.io/learn/30x500/sales-safari)
- The same lesson does not list worldview, tools, "what they have tried" or "what they pay for" as separate template fields *(summary)*. — [egghead.io 30x500 lesson](https://egghead.io/learn/30x500/sales-safari)
- The lesson insists volume is not the point: "Sales Safari is not an optimized workflow because it's more about how your brain processes the data you collect." and "You'll NEVER STOP Sales Safaring." — [egghead.io 30x500 lesson](https://egghead.io/learn/30x500/sales-safari)
- The paid Sales Safari 101 workshop page promises "rules for ferreting out the most wily and subtle of pain points" and "rules for the types of content, categories for the data you find", and ships "Safari 101 Cheat Sheets" plus exercises named "This Old House" and "Amazon Cat Litter" and Quora examples ("Worst Design", "CSS so frustrating"). Price $99 versus 30x500 "normally $1999". The category list itself is not on the public page. — [Sales Safari 101 Workshop, shop.stackingthebricks.com, undated](https://shop.stackingthebricks.com/sales-safari-101)

**What "pain" means in the method**
- "a wide range of negative emotions, which could be things including struggle, confusion, disagreement, debate." Pain shows up as "emotions and patterns." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Questions count as pain: "It's not just because they don't know, there's a reason behind why they don't know." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Complaints and help requests both count, but differently: "People go on the internet to complain about things because something hurts." and "There's a difference between complaining and asking for help." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Record the audience's phrasing, not your interpretation: "first and foremost, how are they expressing it? What are their words?" and "Not what is your interpretation of their problem, how do they perceive their problem?" — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Separate research from solutions: "gathering insights into how they see the situation, not what you can do about it." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Two levels of pain. Hoy's step list for the class: "identify good sources of data", "identify the first level of pain (obvious)", "identify the second level of pain (why is this even being talked about? what's the core here? why do they care?)". — [Amy Hoy, "VINTAGE - Sales Safari in Action", 16 Jan 2014 (videos filmed 2013)](https://stackingthebricks.com/vintage-sales-safari-in-action/)
- In the Year of Hustle free lessons, Sales Safari is "for systematically finding pain in your audience" and questions are "a great first indicator of pain". — [Year of Hustle, free 30x500 Academy lessons, undated (© 2010-2019 Amy Hoy)](https://stackingthebricks.com/year-of-hustle/marketing-content-ebombs/)
- Hidden pain workbook *(search snippet only, unofficial copy behind login)*: the Hidden Pain stage "includes a list of painful emotions the audience might be experiencing" and a checklist question "Did your notes include any pain from OP's follow up comments, not just their original post?" — [30x500 Hidden Pain Virtual Hot Seat Workbook (unofficial mirror)](https://notes.c.devon.lol/Devon/attachments/30x500-Hidden-Pain-Virtual-Hot-Seat-Workbook-and-Cheat-Sheet.pdf)

**What "jargon" is for**
- Beyond copywriting, jargon is a search input: "Compile a specialized glossary for your field and use it as search input" *(summary)*; prompts include how the audience describes problems, named techniques, what they call their customers, "special processes such as 'Inbox Zero'", and "which other roles weigh in on decisions and purchases" *(summary)*. — [Alex Hillman, "How to find your audience's online watering holes", 1 Jun 2017](https://stackingthebricks.com/cant-find-audience/)
- Jargon workbook *(search snippet only, unofficial copy behind login)*: jargon is noted in two groups, "terms you recognize and terms you don't", and "what each term means to that audience in context". — [30x500 Jargon Virtual Hot Seat Workbook (unofficial mirror)](https://notes.c.devon.lol/Devon/attachments/30x500-Jargon-Virtual-Hot-Seat-Workbook-and-Cheat-Sheet.pdf)

**What "worldview" is (from the free 30x500 lesson excerpt, Lessons 26-27)**
- "Your worldview is made up of a million of these little judgments—some kneejerk, some from your gut, some you grew up with, and some you decided carefully based on intellectual analysis of the world." — [30x500 "Why You Should Ditch Niches & Embrace Worldviews" PDF, undated](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- "Worldviews beat niches every time" and "Niches usually define groups of people by slots and numbers: middle-aged housewives, young men with disposable income..." and "Everybody's obsessed with finding a niche when what they should be doing is expressing a worldview." — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- "Of course, the funny trick about these worldviews is that customers treat them as if they're facts. It's not I feel that, but This is how it is." — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- Where worldview notes come from: "Identify which worldviews your desired customers (inside your Audience) tend to hold. Put your Sales Safari skills & research to work!" and "When in doubt, refer back to all that research you collected on your Sales Safari. Or lurk in the watering holes again to refresh your memory." — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- Worldview probe categories the workbook lists: graphic design/visual appearance; "What a person should do with his/her life"; "Buying things—how, why, when, how you feel about it, for personal and for business"; "Fortune, luck, hard work, success, personal connections"; "Business rules, style, & ethics, the way customers or employees should be treated"; "How to tackle something when you don't already know how to do it". — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- "Worldviews are not icing, they're foundation." — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- Customer Physics (why hidden pain exists): real customers "are rarely in the mindset of looking for solutions to their problems (if they even know they have a problem)". — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)

**Watering holes and the audience's tools and spending (the method's own words)**
- Definition in the lesson: "Places that your audience naturally congregates in your community to talk amongst themselves." Types listed: forums, mailing lists, Google groups; meetups and in-person communities with online extras; industry magazines, news sites, link aggregators; blogs and their comments; LinkedIn, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram communities; product support forums; code collaboration tools; product review sites *(summary of list)*. Finding them: "Combine these terms with your audience name and choice jargon." with modifiers "forum," "group," "help," "reviews" and "Try plugging different combinations into google & see what new source you find!" — [egghead.io 30x500 lesson](https://egghead.io/learn/30x500/sales-safari)
- Hillman's description: places "where your audience gathers to ask, complain, rant, rave, share, and lurk for knowledge", namely "forums, discussion lists, blog comments, subreddits, Facebook groups, Twitter hashtags and chat rooms". — [Hillman, watering holes, 1 Jun 2017](https://stackingthebricks.com/cant-find-audience/)
- Three techniques: self-descriptions (brainstorm "at least 5-10 different ways" the audience describes itself and combine with a "21 Watering Hole search terms" cheat sheet), jargon and tools (list the tools "they buy or receive from employers" across software, hardware, books/magazines, conferences), and lateral search (infer membership from adjacent purchases and media) *(summary)*. — [Hillman, watering holes](https://stackingthebricks.com/cant-find-audience/)
- "What stuff do they buy?" is an explicit question, and the purpose is that "their actions will tell you what they need, want, and are ready to buy". Also: "You're not accessing these watering holes to sell to them." — [Hillman, watering holes](https://stackingthebricks.com/cant-find-audience/)
- "You might find 20 dead ends" before a promising source; some places "aren't very good for discovering pain". — [Hillman, watering holes](https://stackingthebricks.com/cant-find-audience/)
- The Worldviews excerpt's glossary: "Sales Safari is half market research, half in-depth analysis. The Sales Safari forms the basis of everything you need to do to ship a product that will earn you money from day 1: choosing the Audience, locating their hangouts, analyzing their worldviews, and then turning that research into a big list of potentially profitable products." — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)
- The 2013 Noko copywriting essay lists the Safari steps as: find the audience where they already gather; observe "what they complain about, share, recommend, and assume, not just what they say when asked"; analyze; "Identify the inverse of their problems"; build pitch and product around that *(summary of numbered list)*. — [Amy Hoy, "How I increased conversion 2.4x with better copywriting", 12 Aug 2013](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-i-increased-conversion-2-4x-with-better-copywriting/)

### Inferences
- The four categories are deliberately coarse. "Tools they use", "what they have tried" and "what they pay for" are captured inside Recommendations ("what to buy, read, share, try... or NOT to") and inside the second level of pain (workarounds), not as separate columns. A pipeline that extracts only "complaints" is capturing one quadrant of four.
- The method's unit of observation is the thread or post read in full, including follow-up comments by the original poster, with multiple passes per thread. It is not a per-sentence classifier.
- "Worldview" has no analogue in any of the automated tools reviewed below; the closest is sentiment, which the method would consider a different thing (worldview is a belief treated as fact, not an emotion).

### Gaps
- The official cheat sheets and the "list of painful emotions" in the Hidden Pain workbook are paywalled; only the category names and two checklist questions were visible via search snippets. The exact wording of the worldview and recommendations columns in the current 30x500 note sheet could not be verified from a public page.
- No public page defines the "few others that show up from time to time" categories Hillman mentions.

## Key question 2: How does the method separate a pain worth building for from noise? Audience-first, money, and how many observations

### Takeaway
The method filters at the audience level first (pick a group that already buys on value and gathers online), then at the pain level by pattern ("what's showing up here a bunch on its own") and by depth (first-level vs second-level pain, workarounds as evidence). It gives no numeric threshold for how many observations make a pattern; the authors say "you just need enough" and that patterns "jump out at you", and the one concrete number in the free lessons is "at least ten audience questions" per hour-long session as a starting point for ebombs, not for products.

### Cited Findings

**Audience first, not idea first (the authors' own words)**
- "A 'problem' is a terrible thing to build a business on." and "The most crucial ingredients are Who? and How Much?" and "Pick an audience that buys things. One you can study, reach, and persuade." then "figure out what they need and want." The salon-booking example: founders noticed salons used paper books, built software, "Those salons didn't want to pay for software" *(summary)*. — [Amy Hoy, "The Pizza Crust Problem, or...the Wrong Way to Find a SaaS Idea", 29 Oct 2019](https://stackingthebricks.com/pizza-crust-problems/)
- "An idea is a terrible thing to build a business on." Ideas go "dead last". Product concepts: "These are not ideas; they don't come from inspiration or the inexplicable firing of neurons; they come from an application of a logical system that matches up pains and solutions." "No burst of genius required." The needs come from "research, volume, [and] observed behavior", not "guesswork or supposition". — [Amy Hoy, "Screw your ideas before they screw you (part 2)", 1 Jan 2014](https://stackingthebricks.com/bootstrapping-series-2-screw-your-ideas-before-they-screw-you/)
- Three questions for product-market fit *(search snippet only)*: "Who am I serving? What do they need/want, and are they ready to buy? How can I reach them and persuade them?" — [Amy Hoy, "How do you create a product people want to buy?", 2013](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-do-you-create-a-product-people-want-to-buy/)
- The 30x500 course page: "No customer = no product." and it rejects "'great idea' spray & pray"; the audience is "surrounded by solid gold market research, it's just invisible." — [30x500 Academy sales page, undated](https://30x500.com/academy/)
- Audience is "much, much broader than a niche" and the system outline runs: find & choose a good Audience → get to know their Needs → learn to understand their Worldview → define their and your Constraints → craft a compelling Offer. — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)

**Choosing a community that has money and buys software (the authors' own words)**
- The 30x500 "is it right for me" page lists audiences that do not work: local small businesses (salons, restaurants, bars): "many baby businesses have died on those shores"; music venues, yoga fans, artists; college students except "the most demanding and valuable majors (engineering, science, law, etc)"; large enterprise buyers with "6-18 month sales cycles"; audiences known for complaining, laziness, poor business skills or being broke: "Run away, as fast as your little legs can carry you."; audiences that do "driveby bitching", suffer silently, or ignore value arguments; audiences that spend on equipment but not on software or education; stingy audiences *(summary with quotes)*. — [Stacking the Bricks, "Is 30x500 Academy right for me?", undated](https://stackingthebricks.com/30x500/is-30x500-right-for-me/)
- Audiences that work: gather online in "watering holes" (forums, blogs, mailing lists, user groups, social groups, review sites); seek solutions (read, share, try, buy); the core test is "Do they buy on value?"; the method "works best on audiences that behave in a business-like way"; developers do buy: students have earned "well over $1 million selling tools and education to developers"; a large mixed audience works "even if only 10–30% match" *(summary with quotes)*. — [Is 30x500 right for me?](https://stackingthebricks.com/30x500/is-30x500-right-for-me/)
- Hillman prefers B2B: "Solving this problem will either put money back in my pocket or stop the bleeding." and "consumer audiences are fickle and really inconsistent." and the target is "people who behave like businesses or businesses". — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/); [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Belong to the audience: "Serving audiences that you belong to is key." but "you are not your audience." — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- On a podcast *(search snippet only)*: "Pick a customer base, make the best to do list app for a specific category of business and ideally a category that you belong to. That's where you're going to have inherent advantages". — [Stacking the Bricks podcast listing](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep16-how-do-you-design-products-people-love/)
- Hoy's paid-signal litmus test: she describes being willing to "spend $200 or $300 on a video class on weaving" as "the litmus test for me if I want to do it after I've done one thing", with the caveat "Granted, I have more disposable income than the average person." — [EP12, "I'm shipping ebombs, now what?", 5 Mar 2015](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)

**Real pain vs noise (the authors' own words)**
- Pattern over single pain: "It's not about finding one pain and locking onto that" and "Instead you observe the entire landscape and you go, what's showing up here a bunch on its own." and "Inconsistencies is another kind of pattern." — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Intensity proxy: "how much something needs to hurt in order for you to go on the internet". — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Workarounds are pain evidence: "Sometimes it's all the things they're doing around it, that aren't solving it." (Hoy). — [EP12](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)
- Vague pain is not usable: "Something sucking is not specific. Something not sucking is equally unspecific." (Hillman); look for "the most specific problems, the problem use cases". — [EP12](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)
- Immediate vs core pain: list posts address "the most superficial (immediate) pains of all"; a student's pains were "much closer to a core pain". Hidden pain signals: "something deep down is leaving them dissatisfied"; "Or they are in a crisis."; people "already in the process of changing" are easier to serve; and if success depends on changing a belief, "you're probably screwed." "Epiphanies can't be forced". — [Amy Hoy, "My customers don't know they have a problem…", 13 Feb 2014](https://stackingthebricks.com/my-customers-dont-know-they-have-a-problem/)
- The method does not require an empty market: "people get obsessed with finding an unmarked slot. It's not necessary." (Hoy). — [EP12](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)
- Saturation fear is "BS"; total addressable market is "a big, sexy, misleading number"; "You have to make exclusive sales" and "You need to bypass the Google problem entirely." — [Amy Hoy, "Competition? SEO? Saturation?! How to Survive + Thrive", 4 Aug 2017](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-to-survive-and-thrive/)

**How many observations before a pattern counts**
- No fixed number. "On one hand it's volume. So you just need enough." and "you have more evidence than you realize you do." (Hillman); "you need to build up enough research and experience from paying attention to the audience", "some trends and themes jump out at you", sometimes "let it stew a little while and come back to it." (Hoy). — [EP12](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)
- Practice is per thread: "We send you into one thread and say, I want you to come away with as many notes as you can" and "a session counts as practice." — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Free-lesson starting quota: browse chosen watering holes for about an hour and collect "at least ten audience questions" in their own words, saved with links into "Sales Safari notes"; then answer two of them *(summary)*. The lesson says this data does not yet tell you what product to build: "Of course not." "But you DO have enough data to start dropping Ebombs, and building a connection to your audience." — [Year of Hustle lessons](https://stackingthebricks.com/year-of-hustle/marketing-content-ebombs/)
- Process is iterative: "collect wide swaths of information, look for emergent patterns and then refine, and then kind of repeat that." — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- Course scale claims (self-reported, unaudited): "developed over 8+ years" with "1,000+ students", "$10+ million in combined student earnings", "9 out of 10 failures in our class are the result of not doing ANY steps." — [30x500 Academy page](https://30x500.com/academy/)
- First practice is on an assigned audience, not your own: "instead of sending people into their own audience for the first practice" (a 2013 change); freelancers were chosen "because even if you're not a freelancer" you have seen the situations. — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/); [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)

### Inferences
- The method's signal/noise filter is mostly upstream of the text: a complaint from an audience that fails "Do they buy on value?" is noise regardless of its intensity. Automated tools that score the complaint without scoring the community invert this order.
- "Recurrence across independent people" and "presence of workarounds" are the two in-text signals the authors name; both are checkable by a pipeline. "Second-level pain" (why do they care) is the one that requires reading the thread, not classifying it.
- The absence of a numeric threshold is a design choice, not an omission: the lesson says the method "is more about how your brain processes the data you collect" than about volume.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives an hours-of-observation or posts-per-pattern number for product decisions. The "at least ten questions in about an hour" figure is for the first ebombs only.
- The phrase "we don't validate ideas, we validate audiences" was not found in any Hoy/Hillman page; the closest primary statements are "Validation is backwards" (2014) and "Pick an audience that buys things" (2019).

## Key question 3: What does the method say to do with the data after collection?

### Takeaway
Collected pain becomes, in order: ebombs (free, single-pain/single-fix educational pieces), a mailing list via a "Call to Action ladder", then a tiny first paid product that addresses pain "as pattern", then a second product for existing customers. The pitch for each uses "Pain, Dream, Fix" and a CTA. The authors reject validation as "backwards" and replace it with observation of behavior, including watching whether ebombs get read, used and shared.

### Cited Findings

**Ebombs**
- The course page: "bite-sized marketing magnets (we call them Ebombs)", used to reach the audience and grow an email list; the overall sequence is "doing Sales Safari, shipping Ebombs, backwards planning, and actually launching." Structure: "Part 1: Read Their Minds" and "Part 2: Build the Business". — [30x500 Academy page](https://30x500.com/academy/)
- Definition by function: "Ebombs are really just tiny fixes; each Ebomb is the antidote to a tiny, specific pain your audience has." Rules: "Single, specific pain. Single, specific fix."; answer only the question asked; "Resist overcomplicating and overthinking."; a paragraph or two "written like an email, addressed to the person who asked" *(summary)*. — [Year of Hustle lessons](https://stackingthebricks.com/year-of-hustle/marketing-content-ebombs/)
- Ebombs are "content that helps people" and the trust they build is "the basis of an easy sale". — [Hoy, "How to Survive + Thrive", 4 Aug 2017](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-to-survive-and-thrive/)
- Ebomb vs product: "Ebombs don't address patterns; products generally address pain as pattern." (Hillman) and "It's framework versus a specific, very specific fix." (Hoy). — [EP12](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep12-im-shipping-ebombs-now-what-from-pain-to-product-with-nick-piegari/)
- Ebomb performance is itself the test: research is used to "create marketing content the audience engages with ('read, use, share')" and "Use that content to bootstrap the first product" *(summary with quote)*. — [Hoy, "Sales Safari in Action", 16 Jan 2014](https://stackingthebricks.com/vintage-sales-safari-in-action/)

**The ladder (the authors call it a "Call to Action ladder"; "product ladder" is not their term)**
- Four positioning tools: "Sales Safari", "Ebomb", "Call to Action ladder" (move people from reading, to sharing, to joining a mailing list, to investing time in a free offer, before asking them to buy *(summary)*), and "Pitch, with the Pain-Dream-Fix formula". — [Hoy, "How to Survive + Thrive", 4 Aug 2017](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-to-survive-and-thrive/)
- Product sequence on the course page: "tiny, free products" (Ebombs) → "Your first PAID product will take more work" → "Introduce a second product, especially one that your existing happy customers are likely to buy." and the first product should be one "you can ship in weeks instead of months". — [30x500 Academy page](https://30x500.com/academy/)
- Portfolio of small products: "Taking a portfolio approach of small products is our number one recommendation."; early products should be "fast to ship, easy to maintain, easy to support"; "Software is very, very hard." and is "the steepest hill to climb". — [EP42](https://stackingthebricks.com/podcast/ep42-what-is-sales-safari-with-eteinne-garbugli/)
- Generation step: from research, generate "Easily 20-40" product concepts, then write pitches for the most viable; stages "Research, Generate, Write, Choose, and Build". — [Hoy, "Screw your ideas", 1 Jan 2014](https://stackingthebricks.com/bootstrapping-series-2-screw-your-ideas-before-they-screw-you/)
- Filter the options: generate variations of the problem, then "you eliminate those, what you're left with is a bunch of options" (filtering for sense, feasibility, audience demand); solutions "come later. That's an entirely separate part of the machinery." — [Lean B2B interview](https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-hillman-sales-safari/)
- The economics anchor: "$30/month worth of value to 500 customers to gross $180k/year." — [30x500 Academy page](https://30x500.com/academy/); same figure in [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)

**Pain, Dream, Fix**
- "Pain, Dream, Fix — aka the reader's pains, the reader's dream, and then how a product will bridge that gap". "You reawaken their pain; then you get them daydreaming of a reality where that pain doesn't even exist." "Then, and only then, talk about the product." The graphic is titled "Pain, Dream, Fix and CTA". — [Hoy, "How I increased conversion 2.4x", 12 Aug 2013](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-i-increased-conversion-2-4x-with-better-copywriting/)
- The dream is derived from the pain: turn the pain, using a process, "into what people want, their dream" *(summary)*. — [Hoy, "Sales Safari in Action"](https://stackingthebricks.com/vintage-sales-safari-in-action/)
- Worldview feeds the pitch: "Right People is not a value judgment, it's a measure of resonance." and the four steps "Marketer, know thyself / know thy customer / make a Venn Diagram / create a consistent, magnetic message". — [30x500 Worldviews PDF](https://stackingthebricks.com/free/30x500-Worldviews.pdf)

**The validation stance**
- Validation "is as much of a system as throwing spaghetti against a wall to see what sticks" and "the results are neither predictably nor repeatably successful." Zoo analogy: "You wouldn't make a judgement call about what MOST lions do based on a lion in a zoo." Interviewees pre-launch "have not yet given you money, and are ergo not customers." Instead: "the answers come from how people behave, even (especially) when you're not in room asking questions." — [Alex Hillman, "Validation is backwards", 2 Apr 2014](https://stackingthebricks.com/validation-is-backwards/)
- Course page: "Validation is backwards" is "a technique for the land of wishin' and hopin'"; "It all comes from your Sales Safari data." — [30x500 Academy page](https://30x500.com/academy/)
- "Sales Zoo" is the contrast term: asking "What is your biggest problem?" is zoo research. — [Hoy, "How I increased conversion 2.4x"](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-i-increased-conversion-2-4x-with-better-copywriting/)
- The course describes itself as "not customer interviews, not cold-calling, not surveys, or validation" and as ethnography: "observing what people do and say when they don't know that they're being asked or observed" *(search snippet only)*. — [30x500 Pioneers page](https://courses.30x500.com/p/30x500-pioneers)

### Inferences
- The method's "validation" is behavioral and happens after collection: ebomb read/share rates and list sign-ups are the measurement, and a paid product is launched only to people already on the list. There is no step where a clustered pain is scored and ranked numerically; ranking is by specificity, feasibility and "fast to ship".
- "Product ladder" in the brief corresponds to two things in the sources: the Call to Action ladder (free content → list → free offer → buy) and the product sequence (ebomb → tiny paid product → second product).

### Gaps
- No public page shows the full "Ebomb recipe" (it is a paid video), nor the criteria list used in the "Choose" stage beyond "fastest to build" and "helps customers most".
- The expansion of "Ebomb" (commonly "education bomb") was not confirmed on any Hoy/Hillman page; searches for the expansion returned nothing relevant.

## Key question 4: How do the 2024-2026 automated tools score and rank what they find?

### Takeaway
Only two vendors publish a scoring scheme with numbers: PainOnSocial (a 0-100 additive rubric over frequency, engagement, emotional intensity, solution-seeking and willingness-to-pay, with no evaluation) and BigIdeasDB (per-cluster severity /5, market gap /10 and frequency kept separate, backed by a published LLM-extraction benchmark against a 900-record human gold standard that found Reddit is the worst source, mean F1 0.599). GummySearch (shutting down) classified posts into themes and exposed engagement; IdeaBrowser outputs 1-10 scores on opportunity/problem/feasibility/timing plus keyword volume and growth but publishes no formula; Exploding Topics, Glimpse and SparkToro are trend and audience tools, not complaint miners, and publish labels rather than formulas.

### Cited Findings

**PainOnSocial (Reddit; 2025-2026)**

Bands: 0–30 Low, 31–50 Moderate, 51–70 High, 71–100 Critical; validate 70+ immediately. — [PainOnSocial severity post](https://painonsocial.com/blog/how-to-score-pain-point-severity-from-reddit)

**BigIdeasDB (formerly SaaSMilli; multi-source)**
- Data sources (July 16, 2026 snapshot) with the vendor's own limitation per source: Capterra reviews 270,000+ ("A review is not a structured pain point"); Capterra structured pain points 39,000+ ("AI-extracted, severity-scored complaints"); Capterra feature gaps 40,000+ ("A request is not proof of willingness to pay"); G2 reviews/insights 129,000+/7,900+; App Store and Google Play reviews 136,000+ of which 99,000+ rated 1-3 ("Collection over-samples negative reviews; not an organic negative rate"); "Reddit structured pain points 2,070 — Community complaints — Directional, not payment validation"; Upwork jobs 5,351 ("Paid-demand signals (pain-point frequency)"; budgets not published); TrustMRR startups 8,000+; funded companies 17,000+; Stripe Index 30,000+; "Swiper validation cards 71,000+ — Human validation signals — A swipe is interest, not purchase intent". — [BigIdeasDB, "Research Methodology and Data Sources", last updated 16 Jul 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/research/methodology)
- Score definitions: "Severity (scored out of 5) measures how frustrated users are in the underlying complaints. A score above 4.0 means people describe the problem in urgent terms, name the money or hours it costs them, and are actively seeking a replacement rather than venting." "Market gap (scored out of 10) measures how poorly the existing tools in a category serve the complaint. A 9.0 to 10.0 means users have tried what is on the market and it still fails them; a 7.0 means partial solutions exist but leave a clear wedge open." "Affected-company count refers to tracked software vendors exhibiting a systemic complaint, not the number of end users." — [BigIdeasDB methodology](https://bigideasdb.com/research/methodology)
- Sampling caveat: "Aggregated cluster and category analyses sample within token limits (roughly several hundred records per cluster) rather than reading every row, so cluster-level figures are representative estimates, not exhaustive counts." — [BigIdeasDB methodology](https://bigideasdb.com/research/methodology)
- Stated limitations: "Complaint data is a demand signal, not a business plan; it does not count competitors or estimate acquisition cost." "Negative sentiment is over-sampled by design in review datasets." Regulated categories carry costs the scores do not capture; self-reported revenue (e.g., Reddit) is unaudited. — [BigIdeasDB methodology](https://bigideasdb.com/research/methodology)
- Unit of analysis: "A pain point is more than a single complaint - it is a cluster of related complaints rolled up with scoring so you can compare problems on equal footing." Each carries severity ("scored by AI"), frequency, impact ("the downstream cost or consequence"), and quotes. The Pain Points database is aggregated from Reddit and Capterra; "When the same pain shows up in both, the frequency signal is far stronger than volume on a single site." — [BigIdeasDB docs, "Pain point analysis", 9 Jul 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/pain-point-analysis)
- Ranking rule: "Rank pain points by frequency and severity together. A frequent but mild annoyance rarely justifies a product; a rare but catastrophic problem may be too niche." The "four-part signal": "high frequency, high pain intensity (4+/5), a quantified cost, and weak existing solutions." Gap detection by phrasing: "the quotes are full of workarounds, spreadsheets, and 'I gave up and just do it manually.' That phrasing is the sound of a gap." — [BigIdeasDB pain point analysis](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/pain-point-analysis)
- Why frequency is kept separate: "a rare but agonizing problem reads very differently from a common but mild annoyance, and collapsing them into one figure would hide exactly the distinction you need." and "A complaint that recurs across hundreds of independent sources is a fact about the market; a complaint you saw once is an anecdote." — [BigIdeasDB docs, "How BigIdeasDB works", 9 Jul 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/how-bigideasdb-works)
- Decision funnel: "Frequency and pain select what to research; interviews, paid pilots, revenue, funding, and supply data test whether the opportunity is commercially credible." Four steps: establish demand → confirm willingness to pay (Upwork jobs, paid tools) → prove the market monetizes (TrustMRR, Funded DB, Stripe Index) → find the gap. Three things it "deliberately avoids": chasing hype, "Solving a problem nobody pays for", "Trusting a single loud source". "An empty niche with no revenue and no companies is the real warning sign." Capterra opportunity dataset: 3,000+ opportunities with "overall opportunity scores up to 8.7/10 and competitive-gap scores up to 9.5/10". — [How BigIdeasDB works](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/how-bigideasdb-works)
- Published evaluation ("LLM Wars", Om Patel, 5 Jul 2026): 10 models, "12,000 records were stratified from a raw corpus of roughly 50,000" across G2, Capterra, App Store/Google Play, Reddit; gold standard of 900 records from "7,920 swipe judgments" by "958 validators" over 70 days with majority vote; "Inter-validator agreement averaged 84.7%"; "12.2% approval rate". A true positive requires "a cosine similarity of the 1,536-dimensional text-embedding-3-small vectors at or above 0.60 and an exact domain-tag match". Composite "F1 × 0.60 + Agreement × 0.40". Output schema: "JSON with at most two pain points per record, each carrying a description, a 1–5 severity, a ≤15-word evidence quote, and a domain tag"; zero-shot, one fixed prompt. — [BigIdeasDB, "LLM Wars: Benchmarking 10 LLMs for Pain Point Extraction (2026)", 5 Jul 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/llm-benchmark-pain-point-extraction)
- Benchmark results: Claude Haiku 4.5 #1 composite 0.7983 (F1 0.8176, P 0.8120, R 0.8234); Claude Sonnet 4.6 highest F1 0.8293 (P 0.9115, R 0.7607); Mixtral 8x7B lowest F1 0.6018. "Every model performed worst on Reddit — average F1 0.599 vs 0.808 on Capterra, a 20.9-point drop." Mean F1 by source: Capterra 0.8083, App Store/Play 0.7778, G2 0.7622, Reddit 0.5990. Sonnet on Reddit: F1 0.6449, recall 0.5113; Haiku on Reddit 0.7487. Cost: Grok-4-fast $4.79 vs Sonnet 4.6 $78.69 for 12,000 records. Reason given: "Reddit users rarely state problems directly" (example: "every Monday I spend an hour manually copying stuff into a spreadsheet because apparently nobody thought export was important"), plus "off-topic replies, meta-discussion, sarcasm, and community humor that sounds like a complaint but is not". "ui-ux was the top category for 7 of the 10 models". Limitations: single zero-shot prompt, early-2026 model snapshot, English-only, some models did not finish all 12,000 records, "about 480 of 958 validators drove most of the 7,920 judgments". — [LLM Wars benchmark](https://bigideasdb.com/llm-benchmark-pain-point-extraction)
- Pipeline recommendation from the benchmark: "A two-stage extract-then-verify pipeline — a high-recall model to cast a wide net, a high-precision model to confirm — can beat any single model" and "Treat informal sources differently. Reddit needs the most robust models ... or extra post-processing". — [LLM Wars benchmark](https://bigideasdb.com/llm-benchmark-pain-point-extraction)
- Vendor caveat: all BigIdeasDB claims are self-published by the company (Pavado Technologies Inc.); the benchmark author is its founder.

**Other LLM-based Reddit miners with a published method**
- redship.io (vendor of RedShip, $29/month): "pain-point intensity score" = sum of five 1-10 dimensions: Frequency; Cross-community (several subreddits vs one); Existing solutions (10 = obvious failure, 3 = barely fall short); Willingness to pay (money offered vs free workarounds); Recency (last 30 days = 10, two years old = 2). Thresholds: 35+ "worth building for", 20-35 "worth tracking", under 20 "move on". A separate five-signal check: 100+ upvotes, 50+ comments, multiple threads, cross-subreddit, threads from the past 90 days (4-5 hits = validated theme). Claims "I wish there was a tool that..." converts "at the highest rate" with no data; also warns Reddit research is not enough for market sizing or willingness to pay "because stated intent overstates purchasing" *(summary)*. No validation data. — [redship.io, Axel Schapmann, 19 May 2026](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-pain-point-research.md)
- MonetScope (Benjian Dai, free beta): scores "pain / urgency / market size / execution difficulty", each with "the actual Reddit quotes that sparked it, plus a rough MVP plan". Builder's findings: "Pain ≠ demand. Threads where everyone angrily agrees 'someone should build X' almost never convert. Real demand sounds smaller and more specific — 'I currently use Notion + 3 spreadsheets and it breaks every Tuesday' is a buyable problem. 'Why is no one solving X!' is not."; "AI is surprisingly bad at distinguishing 'rant' from 'need' out of the box. A generic 'summarize this thread' prompt will tell you every thread is a goldmine. I had to build explicit scoring for pain signal, demand signal, and willingness-to-pay before outputs got useful."; niche subs (r/MedicalCoding, r/SmallFarms, Etsy sellers) "have 10x less noise and 10x more specific workflow language" vs r/SaaS and r/Entrepreneur; "Reddit has the most raw emotional language"; X "is mostly noise". Rough split: "~60-70% of opportunities surfaced are 'pure complaint' (people venting, no documented workaround), 20-30% have explicit workaround signals", and he is "Adding 'workaround mentioned: y/n' as a scoring dimension". — [Indie Hackers, "I spent a month building a tool that reads Reddit/HN for startup ideas", 29 Apr 2026 (comments to 17 May 2026)](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-spent-a-month-building-a-tool-that-reads-reddit-hn-forstartup-ideas-heres-what-real-pain-actually-looks-like-d961673b21)
- WhittleOS (Boris): does not source Reddit; "Our published run pulled 56 documented problems from 41 sources and read 24 pages in full, and the hosts were review and alternatives sites, revenue comparables, job boards and Hacker News threads." Of 56 problems, "32 carry a link you can open and the rest are labelled as our estimate". Dedup: "at URL level yes, at claim level no"; cross-run store keyed on "a hash of the normalised theme plus the problem text", so "two paraphrases of one original complaint do not merge, and five pages restating one source are five records"; the "persistence" counter "is closer to 'this phrasing recurred' than 'five people independently hit this'". — [Indie Hackers, "Four ways mining Reddit for startup ideas went wrong for me, and one disclosure", 18 Sep 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/four-ways-mining-reddit-for-startup-ideas-went-wrong-for-me-and-one-disclosure-307e18f861)
- IdeaScanner (ideacrystal.com) proposes a go/no-go checklist instead of a score: demand verified across "at least three sources (e.g., search volume, competitor reviews, user interviews)", a stable search baseline, documented gaps in alternatives, and observed high-friction actions (email sign-ups, pre-orders) *(summary)*. — [dev.to, "Why Reddit Upvotes Are a Dangerous Metric for SaaS Validation", posted 13 Jun (likely 2026)](https://dev.to/ideacrystal/why-reddit-upvotes-are-a-dangerous-metric-for-saas-validation-2dcb)

| Exploding Topics | search, social, forums, news, e-commerce, podcasts | keyword/topic | growth %, Exploding/Regular/Peaked, seasonality, volatility, 12-month forecast | none published |
| Glimpse | search ("the whole internet") | keyword/topic | volume, YoY/MoM growth, forecast | "95%+ backtested accuracy" claim, no method |
| SparkToro | clickstream panel, LinkedIn profiles | audience | attention/affinity to sites, accounts, podcasts | none relevant |

### Inferences
- Every tool that scores complaints uses some blend of the same five signals: recurrence (frequency, cross-community), engagement (upvotes/comments), emotional intensity, evidence of attempted fixes or workarounds, and money talk. Only BigIdeasDB and the MonetScope builder treat "workaround present" as a first-class dimension, which is also the signal Hoy and Hillman name ("all the things they're doing around it").
- The only quantitative evidence on extraction quality (BigIdeasDB's benchmark) is on the extraction step, not on whether scored clusters become paying products. No tool publishes a hit-rate of scored ideas against revenue.
- The structural split between "score the complaint" (PainOnSocial, redship) and "score the complaint, then require external money evidence" (BigIdeasDB, IdeaScanner checklist) mirrors Sales Safari's "Do they buy on value?" gate, moved from audience selection to post-hoc verification.

### Gaps
- GummySearch's classifier (keyword rules vs model) and its "heat score" formula were never documented publicly; the shutdown date is reported as both 30 Nov 2025 and 30 Nov 2026.
- IdeaBrowser publishes no weights, no definition of its 1-10 labels, and no evaluation. The field list comes from a third-party scraper.
- PainOnSocial does not say whether the product uses the blog rubric's weights; several named signals (comment-to-view ratio, awards, trend direction) have no point values.
- No independent (non-vendor) evaluation of any of these tools was found.

## Key question 5: Published critiques of automated Reddit/forum idea mining

### Takeaway
The critiques converge on four failure modes: complaint volume is not demand (posters are a self-selected vocal minority; "An upvote costs nothing"); a niche forum is a segment, not the market; naive dedup counts restated complaints as independent evidence; and LLM summarizers call every thread a goldmine unless forced to score rant vs need. Most critiques are written by people selling a competing tool, and the few numbers cited (e.g. "90% of Reddit-hyped ideas die") are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- Boris (WhittleOS), four failures: hunting novelty ("A novel complaint is usually novel because it belongs to one person with an unusual setup. What is worth finding is the boring complaint that keeps reappearing in different words from people who do not know each other."); not writing findings as sentences with links ("by the fourth thread you already know what you are hoping to see, and you see it"); reading volume as demand ("Threads sample who was annoyed enough to type, not who would pay. Satisfied users never post, so the loudest problem in a community is often the problem of its most vocal minority."); assuming a niche forum represents its market ("A community selects its members, so whatever you conclude generalises to people already there. That is a real segment. It is not the market."). — [Indie Hackers, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/four-ways-mining-reddit-for-startup-ideas-went-wrong-for-me-and-one-disclosure-307e18f861)
- Stefan Pantelich's reply on independence: "Five pages can repeat the same original complaint, quote one another, or reflect the same commercial incentive. That creates the appearance of persistence without five separate people encountering the problem." and the proposed provenance fields: "source type, date, user or buyer role, current workaround, and whether it is genuinely independent of the other evidence." Boris's answer: the achievable version is "count distinct hosts and distinct dates, and say that's what the number means." — [same thread, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/four-ways-mining-reddit-for-startup-ideas-went-wrong-for-me-and-one-disclosure-307e18f861)
- Benjian Dai (MonetScope builder): "The hard part was figuring out which complaints online are actual paying problems, and which are just people venting into the void." and "Pain ≠ demand." (see KQ4 for full quotes). Commenter "3vo": "The tell is always: what are they doing instead? If they have a workaround - a spreadsheet, a manual process, three duct-taped tools - that's the signal. The workaround is proof of willingness to invest effort." and "Reddit/HN mining is good for finding the frustration layer. The next step is finding the workaround layer". Commenter "Wizcard Team": "a problem people mention once is noise, but one they keep coming back to is signal." — [Indie Hackers, 29 Apr 2026 and comments 15-17 May 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-spent-a-month-building-a-tool-that-reads-reddit-hn-forstartup-ideas-heres-what-real-pain-actually-looks-like-d961673b21)
- IdeaScanner (vendor) on upvotes: a comment like "I would pay for this" "can easily be mistaken for a signed contract"; "low-stakes enthusiasm in an anonymous forum has almost no correlation with actual purchasing behavior"; "An upvote costs nothing"; "Reddit is an excellent discovery tool, but it is not a decision engine." Figures in the post ("Approximately 90% of Reddit-hyped ideas die within six months"; 8% of 200 buzzed concepts active after six months; landing-page CTR under 1.8% and trial sign-up under 0.3% from high-upvote posts) are given with no source or method. — [dev.to, 13 Jun (likely 2026)](https://dev.to/ideacrystal/why-reddit-upvotes-are-a-dangerous-metric-for-saas-validation-2dcb)
- IdeaScanner (vendor) on scraping: "Reddit comments surface pain signals, but they cannot confirm market demand." Four blind spots: search volume, trend direction, "Invisible competition" ("locked down by well-funded incumbents"), and pricing: "Complaining about a problem is free. Paying for a solution is not." Also "If there are no competitors, ask why." — [dev.to, 1 Jun (likely 2026)](https://dev.to/ideacrystal/why-reddit-scraping-isnt-enough-for-saas-validation-1cem)
- BigIdeasDB's own caveats double as critique of single-source mining: Reddit structured pain points are "Directional, not payment validation"; a swipe "is interest, not purchase intent"; "Trusting a single loud source - one viral complaint can mislead"; and its benchmark finding that LLM extraction on Reddit has mean F1 0.599 with top models missing "more than half of the real pain points" (Sonnet recall 0.5113). — [BigIdeasDB methodology](https://bigideasdb.com/research/methodology); [LLM Wars](https://bigideasdb.com/llm-benchmark-pain-point-extraction)
- Selection bias statement *(search snippet only)*: "Selection bias is baked in... That is useful for finding edges, misleading for finding averages." — [rawneed.pages.dev FAQ](https://rawneed.pages.dev/faq/)
- Sales Safari's own pre-emptive critique of complaint-only audiences: audiences that do "driveby bitching", "suffer silently", or ignore value arguments are to be avoided, and "people who complain... or being broke": "Run away, as fast as your little legs can carry you." — [Is 30x500 right for me?](https://stackingthebricks.com/30x500/is-30x500-right-for-me/)
- Product Hunt / Indie Hackers comment-thread critiques *(search snippets only; the specific comment pages were not retrieved)*: "scraping reddit for 'i hate when X' gets you a thousand complaints but most aren't painful enough to pay for or they're already solved"; "Reddit pain is real but sometimes it's just venting, so I'm curious how you separate noise from actual buying intent. Have you seen cases where a high 'pain score' actually led to people willing to pay?"; "isn't the bottleneck that most validated pain points on reddit are already being solved by 5 startups? the gap isn't finding pain, it's finding pain that's underserved AND has buyers." — [Product Hunt, Reddit Pain Analyzer listing](https://www.producthunt.com/p/reddit-pain-analyzer/reddit-pain-analyzer); [Indie Hackers short link](https://indiehackers.com/post/b855787052)

### Inferences
- The critiques and Sales Safari agree on the remedy: weight recurrence across independent people over intensity in one thread, treat workarounds and money already spent as the demand signal, and judge the community's buying behavior before judging its complaints.
- Every critic who runs a pipeline reports the same engineering lesson: an LLM must be given explicit, separate scores for pain, demand and willingness-to-pay, or it rates everything as an opportunity (Dai; BigIdeasDB's precision/recall split).
- Claim-level deduplication (merging paraphrases of one complaint, and discounting pages that quote each other) is the critique most directly applicable to a cluster-and-count pipeline, and none of the reviewed tools claims to do it.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or independent study was found that measures whether tool-ranked pain points convert to paying customers; all "accuracy" claims are vendor-authored.
- No Hacker News thread specifically critiquing GummySearch/IdeaBrowser-style tools surfaced in searches; the Product Hunt and Indie Hackers comment quotes above come from search excerpts whose exact pages were not retrieved.
- The "90% die within six months" figure is unsourced and should not be reused.
